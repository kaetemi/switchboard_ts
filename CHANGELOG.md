# Changelog

## 3.0.0-dev.1 (unreleased)

The first version of the TypeScript port of the Switchboard protocol, wire-compatible with the Dart reference implementation (`switchboard` and `switchboard_quic`). ES modules only; Node.js 22 or later, Bun, Deno and browsers. Entry points `@kaetemi/switchboard` (runtime-neutral core), `/node`, `/web` and `/testing`.

- **Primitives and addressing:** status codes and `Status` (byte-exact bodies, MOVED and RELOCATED fields), `SwitchboardError` and `ProtocolError`, 8-byte `Name`s, byte readers and writers, `ServiceAddress` and `ChannelAddress`; an injectable `Clock` and `Logger` in every options object; `ManualClock` for tests.
- **Transports:** the stream binding (preamble and length-prefixed frames, as a codec and as Web Streams transforms) over any byte duplex, an in-memory transport pair and `mem://` endpoints, a WebSocket client over the global `WebSocket`; on Node, Bun and Deno, TCP (`connectTcp`, `listenTcp`), a WebSocket server and client over the optional `ws` package (`listenWebSocket`, `acceptWebSocket`), and `connectTransport` for `tcp`, `ws`, `wss` and `mem` endpoints.
- **Frame mux:** `FrameMuxConnection` and its channels with credit-based flow control, LIMITS, keep-alive, GOAWAY and drain, the output scheduler with its bulk tier, the receive high-water mark, held OPEN payloads; channels as async iterables and Web Streams; the frame and control codecs.
- **Identity:** the credential codec, scopes and rights, `CredentialIssuer` (HMAC-SHA256 and Ed25519), `CredentialVerifier` (offline, cached, key rotation), `Ed25519HolderKey`, the NONCE and IDENT exchange with nonce-bound and exporter-bound proofs, peer identities, over WebCrypto.
- **Talk:** requests, stream requests with item windows and credit, plain messages, aborts, timeouts and EXTEND, ordered answers, bulk payloads on `_bulk` channels with reassembly budgets and streamed delivery (bursts of any size over the QUIC binding too), and `forwardMessage` with flow control passed through hop by hop.
- **The node:** `Switchboard` with service registration and dispatch, channel policies (`ChannelPolicies`, including scoped and identity-requiring policies), the pooled connections, static, endpoint and naming resolvers, identity on connections, CONNECT brokering, slot routing (`openChannelToSlot`, `openTalkToSlot`, `resolveSlotOwner`), the proxy (`proxyHandler`, `pipeChannels`), the relay (`RelayService`, `RelayConfig`), `PeerSet` and `MeshNode`; the Node node listens on TCP, WebSocket, raw QUIC and WebTransport; the browser node connects over WebSocket and WebTransport.
- **Clients:** `ReconnectingClient` with backoff, GOAWAY-aware reconnects and persistent channels (`openPersistent`, `openPersistentTalk`), over a transport connector or a connection connector.
- **Naming and sharding:** the `_ns` protocol, `NamingClient` (registration, the mirrored table, renewal, CONNECT), `NamingService` (leases, watches, brokering, identity scopes, slot spaces, the allocator and migrations), `NamingResolver`, slot tables and keys, `SlotChannel` with the MOVED retry, `SlotGate` and `SlotLifecycle`.
- **QUIC and WebTransport:** the QUIC binding (records, HELLO, the control stream, drain holds, exporter-bound identity, `QuicIdentityRules`), `connectQuic`, `listenQuic`, `quicConnector` and the node binding; a native provider for Node, Bun and Deno (`nativeQuic()`, `selfSignedCertificate`) as an N-API addon over the same Rust QUIC shim the Dart package uses, with prebuilt optional dependencies for Linux x64 and arm64 (glibc and musl), macOS arm64 and x64 and Windows x64; a WebTransport provider for browsers (`webTransport()`, `webTransportConnector`); an in-memory QUIC provider in `/testing`.
- **Tests:** the Dart test suites ported file by file, the wiki test vectors by id, integration tests over memory, TCP, WebSocket and QUIC, browser tests in Chromium and Firefox, regression tests from an adversarial review of every layer (`review-*.test.ts`), and an interop harness that runs the Dart reference and this port as peers of each other, in every pairing over TCP, WebSocket, QUIC and WebTransport (also with the TS peer on Bun). The unit and integration projects run on Node 22, 24 and 26 and on Deno; the unit project on Bun.

### Changes from the adversarial reviews and the shutdown fixes (2026-10-18 to 2026-10-19)

Five independent reviews of the layers (36 findings fixed) and the closing of the two end-of-session races. The wire is unchanged. What a user sees:

- **The end of a QUIC or WebTransport session.** `goAway()` writes a `PING` right after its GOAWAY and closes only once the peer answered it, the connection ended or `closeConfirmTimeout` passed, so the peer reads the GOAWAY before the session goes (the record could be lost with the session before). Against a peer that reads nothing on the control stream, `goAway()` and a node's `close()` can therefore take up to `closeConfirmTimeout` (30 s) instead of `goAwayGrace`, unless the keep-alive ends the connection first. Over WebTransport a channel holds a reset or `STOP_SENDING` that carries no application code for up to 2 s for the session's end, and ends with the session's status (`GOING_AWAY` after a graceful close) where it used to end `UNKNOWN`. These are the choices of the Dart binding (switchboard_quic d509a42) and of the binding page, so the two implementations read the same. A `goAwayGrace` of 0 over QUIC now means no drain, as over the frame mux (it waited without a bound).
- **Bytes only below Talk.** `send`, `open`, `openAfter` and a channel's `writable` throw a `TypeError` for anything but a `Uint8Array`, on the frame mux and the QUIC binding alike (a string went out as zero bytes, and an `ArrayBuffer` left a QUIC channel unable to send or close). Talk and the layers above still take strings.
- **Caller bytes are copied, not kept as views.** An OPEN payload, a `PING` payload, an `identify` intent, DATA held behind an `openAfter`, a write the native provider could not hand to the stack yet and the in-memory provider's write no longer change when the caller reuses a Node `Buffer` after the call.
- **Writables reject.** A write to a `SlotChannel` once its channel can no longer send rejects with `FAILED_PRECONDITION` and errors the `writable`, as on a mux channel; the WebSocket transport's `writable` errors once the socket ended (`writer.closed` settles and later writes reject). `SlotChannel` is async iterable over its subframes, and cancelling its `readable` before the first read reaches the mux channel.
- **Signals added.** `NamingClient.register`, `defineSlots`, `declareHolding`, `claim`, `release` and `locate`, `MeshNode.publish`, `publishSharded`, `claimSlot` and `releaseSlot`, `SlotGate.claim` and `release`, `Switchboard.identifyOn` and `resolveRelays` take an `AbortSignal` (a call not sent yet is withdrawn, a sent `LOCATE` or `CLAIM` is cancelled, a sent `RELEASE` runs to its answer). `connectQuic`'s signal also covers the binding's start. An open aborted with a `SwitchboardError` as its reason rejects with that reason, where it came back as `UNAVAILABLE`.
- **Contract changes.** `MuxOptions.wallTime` (optional) is the clock credential expiry is read on, `peerIdentity` included; a `Switchboard` takes its verifier's. `SwitchboardRuntime.transportPair(clock)` takes the node's clock, so the in-process pairs of a node (`mem://`, its own listeners) run on it. **Renamed exports** (values unchanged, so code that imported the old names gets the other label): the contract's `webTransportExporterLabel` (`switchboard-ident`) is now `webTransportIdentLabel`, and `webTransportTlsExporterLabel` (`EXPORTER-WebTransport`) is now `webTransportExporterLabel`, the names Dart uses.
- **Bounds.** `MeshNode.leave()` bounds each gate's close by `leaveTimeout` (an unload that never ends no longer keeps it, and an `await using` of the mesh node, pending for ever); a gate abandoned that way is logged as a warning. Over QUIC, the unreferenced bulk channels an idle Talk channel admits beyond `maxUnclaimedBulk` are bounded by `reassemblyBudget` (272 at the defaults, where about 4400 were admitted). `talkOptions`, and so a `TalkChannel`, throws a `RangeError` for a `bulkChunkSize` that is not a positive integer (Dart's sender loops on it). `connectQuic`, `listenQuic` and `QuicSwitchboardListener.start` reject with a `RangeError` for binding options out of range before anything is dialled or accepted.
- **Also fixed.** `ping()` and a second concurrent `incoming.next()` reject through pre-caught promises (a dropped heartbeat ping was an unhandled rejection). `close()` on a channel that has ended and that nothing reads releases what it buffered from the receive pause. A wildcard listener is the node's own under the machine's host name. `ReconnectingClient.connected` no longer resolves with a connection a state listener left (one that stops the client or forces a reconnect on `connected`). An endpoint declared while `close()` starts goes with the close, and `adopt` rejects for a connection whose `incoming` is read elsewhere. The `ws` adapter of the WebSocket transports waits for its output on the clock the transport was given, not the system clock, and `sleep` takes a clock as an optional third parameter. `NamingService.close` publishes the slots it frees on `slotEvents`, and the `on()` listeners of the naming client and service run once the step that made the change is done. A Talk channel's graceful close delivers the bulk payloads that arrived whole before it, a bulk channel arriving between a parent's end and its linger reaches its message, and a proxy refuses at once a bulk channel it will not linger for. A provider that throws on a stream's `stop` or `reset` is logged, not thrown out of the channel's `close(status)`.
- **Documented, not changed.** A write queued on a channel's `writable` reaches the channel a microtask or more later, so a `channel.close()` in the same turn goes first: close with `writer.close()`, await the write or use `send()`. A plain Talk message's `signal` never aborts (a request's does). Two questions are open for the maintainer: a dialler that only verifies against a default listener never identifies, and unread channels the peer closed count toward the receive pause (`docs/design.md`, section 13, items 9 and 10).
